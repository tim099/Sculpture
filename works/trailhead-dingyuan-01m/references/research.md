**Cross-check from the drawings [measured]:** with the stern-to-ram-tip length set to 94.5 m, the design profile gives WL length ≈91.4 m, draught ≈6.0 m, and beam/length proportions that match 18.3 m. So **94.5 m is overall length including the ram, and ~91 m is the waterline length.** The "308 ft" figure equals 93.9 m, the same as navypedia's oa.

### 1.2 Hull form (from the 1883 design drawings and photo NH 1926) [measured / inference]
- **Ram bow:** the stem is near-vertical from the deck down to about 2.2 m below WL, then curves forward into a pointed ram. The ram tip is ≈2.7 m ahead of the stem line and ≈3.4 m below WL. The forefoot runs up to the tip in a straight rising line (Brassey's sketch shows the same).
- **Stern:** a rounded counter, near-vertical, with almost no overhang (deck end ≈0.5 m abaft the WL end). In plan it is an elliptical round stern. It has a single rudder behind twin screws (Wright via EN wiki [read]).
- **Plan shape:** long parallel middle body. The forward third tapers to a fine point; the stern is bluff and elliptical. Upper-deck plan L/B ≈4.9.
- **Midship section (frame 30):** flat-bottomed and round-bilged, with near-vertical sides and **essentially no tumblehome** above WL. It has a double bottom ≈1 m deep.
- **Freeboard / deck arrangement** [inference: sections + profile + photo agree with this reading]:
  - **Waist** (≈22 m to ≈69 m from the stern, i.e. between main mast and foremast): the main deck is ≈2.4–2.5 m above WL. A solid bulwark rises above it, with a strake line visible at ≈2.2 m in the photo and a dark plating band to ≈4.4 m, topped by light-coloured hammock netting to ≈5.2–5.5 m. The barbettes, funnels, torpedo boats and hurricane deck are all in this waist.
  - **Poop** (stern to ≈22 m) and **forecastle** (≈69 m to bow) are full-width raised decks at ≈5.2 m above WL. They hold accommodation with windows (upper row of side scuttles ≈3.7–4.1 m above WL).
  - A lower row of side scuttles sits ≈1.7 m above WL, lighting the 'Zwischendeck' between the armour deck (at WL) and the main deck.
  - The external sheer line therefore looks **flush and nearly straight** from stern to bow at ≈5.2 m. Sheer is negligible in all drawings and photos.
- **Breastwork / citadel:** a central armoured citadel ≈43.5 m long (navypedia [read]). On the design plans it is a big rounded rectangle from ≈24.8 m to ≈67.6 m from the stern (42.8 m) [measured]. There is **no raised breastwork** in the Inflexible sense. The barbettes rise from the waist deck, and the ends are unarmoured with an underwater protective deck.
- The ships were reported **wet forward** because of trim from the forward-placed guns (Feng p.33 via EN wiki [read]).

Other details:
- **Barbette vertical stack** (frame 42) [measured]: waist deck 2.4 m → barbette armour top ≈5.0 m → gun axis ≈5.6 m → turret shield roof ≈6.8 m → hurricane deck ≈8.1 m (all above WL).
- **Conning tower:** 8 in armour (all sources). **Position not established** from what I read. Brassey's plan shows a small round structure on the CL just forward of the two barbettes, at the forward end of the Y-shaped passage linking them. Fig. 9 shows a box on the CL ≈73–75 m from the stern, just forward of the foremast. Treat ≈70–75 m on CL, under or forward of the hurricane deck, as unverified.
- **Ventilators:** several cowl ventilators are drawn around the funnels and along the waist. Frame 30 shows two large cowls at the sides rising ≈5–6 m above the deck.
- **Sails:** a temporary transfer rig (Fig. 8, "Ueberführungstakelage") was fitted for the 1885 delivery voyage only, then removed (Wright pp.50–53 via EN wiki [read]). In Fig. 8 the topmasts reach ≈37 m (main) and ≈34 m (fore) above WL. In service the ships carried two military masts with fighting tops, which naval-encyclopedia says were added after the rig came off [read].

## 3. Armament

**Main battery:** 4 × 30.5 cm Krupp in two twin barbette turrets, en echelon amidships-forward.
- Gun type: 30.5 cm MRK L/25 per EN Wikipedia [read]. Other figures in the literature: "20-calibre" (EN Dingyuan article body), "12 in/25" (historyofwar).
- 30.5 cm MRK L/25 article [read]:
  - Total length 7,650 mm (25 cal); bore 6,720 mm.
  - Weight 37.5–38.7 t (Brassey's 1890 says 37 LT for Dingyuan's guns).
  - Charge 100 kg (Zhenyuan's guns, Kiel tests 1884).
  - Muzzle velocity ≈468–500 m/s.
  - Shells for the early model: steel / chilled-iron 333 kg and cast-iron 282 kg. The 455 kg shells are for a later Dutch model.
- **Rate of fire and crew per gun: not found.**
- Shields: thin hoods (historyofwar: "7/8 in"; Wright via EN wiki: 0.5–3 in). The main-gun shields were removed in 1894 because of splinters (naval-encyclopedia, Wright).
- navypedia's 305 mm face / 152 mm side / 76 mm crown figures are for the post-1895 Japanese *Chin Yen*.

**Secondary battery:** 2 × 15 cm Krupp (MRK L/35), one each in single light turrets on the forecastle and poop centrelines. Turret armour 0.5–3 in.

**Light guns** (sources disagree):
- 2 × 47 mm Hotchkiss revolving cannon, plus 8 × 37 mm Maxim-Nordenfelt QF "in casemates" (Feng p.21 via EN wiki).
- zh Wikipedia adds 2 × 57 mm.
- historyofwar: 6 × 37 mm.
- The Weihai replica adds extra guns that were not on the original (hksw.org [read]).

**Torpedo tubes:** 3 × 14 in (356 mm), some sources say 15 in, all above water. One is in the stern and two are broadside forward of the main battery (Wright pp.50–51 via EN wiki [read]). navypedia for Chin Yen says "one bow, two beam".

**Boats:** two 2nd-class torpedo boats (zh wiki says 3).

## 4. Armour (compound steel-on-iron, "康邦鐵甲")

- **Belt:** 14 in (356 mm), over the citadel only, with a citadel length of **43.5 m** (navypedia [read]; Baidu Baike [snippet]; historyofwar gives "144", unit missing).
  - navypedia gives three layers: lower layer 1.5 m high at 203 mm, middle 1.5 m high at 356 mm, upper 254 mm.
  - The frame-30 drawing shows the thick side running from ≈1.5 m below WL up to the main deck at ≈2.4 m [measured, low confidence].
- **Transverse citadel bulkheads:** 356 mm (navypedia [read]).
- **Deck:** 3 in (76 mm). Flat over the citadel at about WL level. At the ends it is a protective deck below WL, turtle-backed forward (navypedia). Wright via EN wiki says the deck ran the full length with unprotected ends.
- **Barbettes:** 12 in (305 mm). Some sources say 12–14 in. A 2020 recovered plate from the barbette area measures 2.86 × 2.60 × 0.33 m and weighs ≈18 t (thepaper/北京青年報 [read]).
- **Conning tower:** 8 in (203 mm).
- **"Casemate" strake:** 8 in (EN wiki).
- **15 cm turrets:** 0.5–3 in.

## 5. Machinery and performance

- **Engines:** 2 horizontal 3-cylinder trunk engines, described as compound (EN class article / Wright [read]; historyofwar). The maker is not stated in what I read; presumably Vulcan.
- **Boilers:** 8 cylindrical fire-tube boilers in 4 boiler rooms, uptaken to 2 funnels.
- **Screws:** 2 shafts. naval-encyclopedia says each drove "a single bronze 3 m propeller" [read]. **Blade count not found.**
- **Power and speed:**
  - Design: 6,000 ihp, 14.5 kn.
  - Trials: Zhenyuan 7,200 ihp / 15.4 kn; Dingyuan "exceeded design" with no figures given (Wright).
  - Other figures in sources: 6,200 / 6,300 / 6,400 hp and 15.7 kn @ 7,500 ihp.
  - **Speed at Yalu: not found.**
- **Coal:** 700 LT normal, 1,000 LT maximum.
- **Range:** 4,500 nm @ 10 kn.
- **Crew:** 363 (Wright). Other sources give 350 and 329.

## 6. Colours and flag

**Hull and upperworks:**
- **From 1887/1888:** Royal-Navy-style livery with black hull, white superstructure, and buff funnels, masts and ventilators. EN Wikipedia gives 1888; naval-encyclopedia gives "after 1887".
- **August 1894:** repainted light grey for the war (both sources [read]). hksw.org says the actual wartime ships were "two-tone grey" and that the replica's Victorian livery is inaccurate [read].
- **Photo NH 1926 (1884–95)** [measured/visual]: dark hull to ≈4.4 m, light/white hammock-netting band on top, light upperworks, mid-tone (buff) funnels.
- **Below waterline:** **not found** in any source read.
- **Before 1887 (as delivered):** **not found**.

**Flag** (EN Wikipedia "Flag of the Qing dynasty" [read]):
- **1862–1889:** triangular yellow dragon flag, proportion 56:87, with a blue (azure) dragon on yellow.
- **1889 (promulgated 1888) to 1912:** rectangular, proportion 2:3. "Blue dragon on plain yellow, with a red pearl at the upper left corner".
- Beiyang admiral and rank flags changed in 1890. That article does not give their designs.

## 8. Measurement method (for re-checking)

I overlaid a pixel grid on the three Commons drawings and the photo, and recorded coordinates in each image's own pixel frame.

**Fig. 10 (profile):**
- Stern at deck x=110; stem at deck x=1464; ram tip x=1506; WL y≈305; keel y≈394.
- Scale: 1396 px ≡ 94.5 m → **14.77 px/m**. The draught comes out at 6.0 m, consistent with the sources.
- Main mast x≈442. Funnels at x 711–747 and 792–830, tops y≈108. Foremast x≈1135.
- Hurricane deck y≈185, x≈830–1135. Upper sheer line y≈228. Waist deck y≈268.

**Fig. 9 (plan):**
- Stern x=75.6; stem x=1415.6. Hull edges y≈751 and 1023, giving a beam of 272 px. Centreline y≈881.
- Port barbette centre (877, 803), starboard (1002, 956), both Ø≈132 px. Bow turret centre x≈1349. Scale ≈14.6 px/m.

**Sections (frames 24, 30, 42):**
- Hull beam 380 px ≡ 18.3 m → 20.8 px/m.
- Keel to deck at side 180 px = 8.6 m. Draught ≈122–129 px = 5.9–6.2 m. Deck ≈2.3–2.5 m above WL.
- Frame 42: barbette top ≈5.0 m above WL, gun axis ≈5.6 m.

**Photo NH 1926:** hull ≈1185 px ≈ 92 m → ≈12.8 px/m, near-broadside. This is a perspective photo, so heights are ±1 m.

**Known conflict:** the photo and Fig. 8 show a high, flush side at ≈5.2 m, while the midship sections show the deck at ≈2.4 m. My reading is a waist deck plus a high bulwark and hammock netting, with raised poop and forecastle, as described in §1.2. Check this against a better source, such as Feng 2018 or Wright, if you can get one.
