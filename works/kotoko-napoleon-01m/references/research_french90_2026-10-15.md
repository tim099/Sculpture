# Le Napoléon (1850): comparison and background data

Purpose: background and comparison data to fill gaps in the Napoléon-specific research. The ship's own specifications are covered by a separate researcher.
Scale context: 0.1 m per voxel.

Labels used:
- **[src]** means the number was read on the cited page.
- **[snippet only]** means the number came from a search-result summary and the page itself was not opened.
- **[derived]** means my own arithmetic from sourced numbers.
- **[observed]** means I looked at a public-domain or CC photo myself.
- **[generic]** means a non-French or non-period rule, used as a fallback.

Units: "ft" in Shipscribe are English feet (Shipscribe gives metric equivalents, e.g. 233ft 8in = 71.23 m).

Armament evolution of the Suffren class, which shows the same gun family Napoléon carried [src: Shipscribe 113a]:
- Design: GD 30 × 30-pdr No.1; UD 32 × 30-pdr No.2; SD 24 × 30-pdr carronades and 4 × 18-pdr.
- Bayard and Duguesclin, c.1850: GD 22 × 30-pdr No.1 and 8 × 22 cm No.1 shell; UD 24 × 30-pdr No.2 and 8 × 22 cm No.2 shell; SD 20 × 30-pdr carronades and 4 × 16 cm shell.

That gives 15 lower-deck ports per side on 60.5 m [derived], against Napoléon's 18 on 71.5 m.

### 2.2 Hull-form features

- **Tumblehome.** The Suffren class "introduced the innovation of having straight walls, instead of the tumblehome design that had prevailed until then". Straight sides "tended to heighten the ships' centre of gravity, but provided much more room ... in the upper decks" [src: Wikipedia, Suffren-class, Design]. A French Navy history (Cols Bleus 1966) says the ship had "la particularité d'être à murailles droites, sans la rentrée habituelle" [snippet only — https://www.colsbleus.defense.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/2018-12/archive_967_26nov66.pdf]. A Napoléon designed in 1847 would plausibly have little or no tumblehome. That is an inference, so confirm it against Napoléon's plans.
  [observed] The Commons photo of the Musée de la Marine Napoléon model (§7) shows near-vertical topsides with only slight inward curve at the rail.
- **Stern.** "Inflexible was completed with a rounded stern which served as a model for conversions of other ships after the late 1840s" [src: Shipscribe 113a]. Henri IV (Hercule class) was "among the first French ships ordered with a rounded stern", and others were fitted with one during construction or steam conversion [src: Shipscribe 112a]. The Tourvilles were "given new-style round sterns" [src: Shipscribe 113a].
  [observed] The Napoléon model shows a **round stern**, with the rudder and an open frame aft. The 1829 Suffren model shows the older square stern with quarter galleries.
- **Bow.** [observed] Both models (Suffren 1829, Napoléon 1850) show a **closed (round) bow** with a knee of the head, headrails and a figurehead, not a beakhead bulkhead. No textual source was found.
- **Sheer.** No numeric source was found. [observed] The models show a moderate, smooth sheer, with the wale and battery lines rising gently toward bow and stern.
- **Port spacing and port size.** No sourced French figure was found; see Gaps.
  [derived estimate] If the lower-deck ports span about 78–80% of deck length: Napoléon 18 ports on 71.5 m gives ≈ 3.2–3.4 m between port centres, and Suffren 15 ports on 60.5 m gives ≈ 3.4 m.
  Rule of thumb only: a port must clear a 165 mm bore gun with its muzzle swell, giving a width of about 0.9–1.0 m. This is NOT sourced.
- **Height between decks.** No sourced figure was found; see Gaps.

## 4. Masts and spars

No French period table was retrieved. The figures below are **[generic]**, from Fincham, *A Treatise on Masting Ships and Mast Making* (1843 ed.), archive.org — https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_z4QhGFh-7ZwC (full text: …/bub_gb_z4QhGFh-7ZwC_djvu.txt).

- **Yard lengths, by nation, as fractions of deck length** ("On Masting Ships", General Observations, pp. 89–91):
  - "in French ships, main-yards .57, top-sail .41, and top-gallant .29 of the length, with the fore-yards seldom exceeding from .89 to .9 the main".
  - English ships: main .55, topsail .44, topgallant .28.
- **Two-deck ships** (supplementary classification, p. ~204):
  - Main yard: .51–.545 × gun-deck length.
  - Main topmast (hounded length): 1.05–1.17 × extreme breadth.
- **Mast positions** (p. ~90):
  - Fore mast: "seldom been carried forward to exceed .4 the length of the water-line from the middle ... seldom been placed nearer to the middle than .34".
  - Main mast: "from .05 to .079 the length of the water-line abaft the middle".
  - Ships: "seldom exceed, for the main, .07 the length abaft, and the fore is frequently .34 before the middle of the water-line" (brig section).
- **Applied to Napoléon** [derived]. Napoléon carried the Jupiter-type 80-gun rig (Shipscribe 133a), so scale from **Jupiter's** dimensions, not Napoléon's: deck 194ft 6in ≈ 59.3 m, extreme breadth 51ft 11in ≈ 15.8 m. Then:
  - French main yard ≈ 0.57 × 59.3 ≈ **33.8 m**; fore yard ≈ 30 m; main topsail yard ≈ 24.3 m; main topgallant yard ≈ 17.2 m.
  - Main topmast (hounded) ≈ 16.6–18.5 m.
  - Mast positions use Napoléon's own waterline (71.2 m): fore mast ≈ 24–28.5 m forward of midships; main mast ≈ 3.6–5.6 m abaft midships. Napoléon's engine room may have shifted these; check the model photo (§7) and the Napoléon-specific plans.
- **Lower mast lengths and diameters, bowsprit angle:** not obtained (see Gaps). Fincham's per-class tables (p. 294 "Table of the Dimensions of Masts, Yards, &c., of English, French, and Swedish Ships") contain them, but the OCR is unreadable. Read the page images in the archive.org viewer.

## 5. Early steam warship features

- **Funnels:**
  - Napoléon had two funnels, the only French ship of the line to do so [src: Shipscribe 133a].
  - [observed] The Musée de la Marine model (23 MG 8, dated 1850) shows **two funnels on the centreline, one just abaft the main mast and one between the main and fore masts**. Treat this as visual evidence, to be confirmed against plans.
  - Algésiras moved the boilers forward of the engines [src: Wikipedia class page], consistent with a single funnel.
- **Telescopic funnels:** St Jean d'Acre (RN, 1853) could "retract its telescoping funnel" [src: Wikipedia]. No source confirms whether Napoléon's funnels telescoped (see Gaps).
- **Lifting screw:**
  - Napoléon and Algésiras had fixed screws; later ships of the group had hoisting screws [src: Shipscribe 133a].
  - Mechanism: a well between the sternpost and rudder post, with "a common two-blade propeller ... hung in a frame, which can be lifted by means of a rope running over two sheaves in the frame and two at the top of the well". Toothed rails on the posts took ratchets that held the frame. One model was inspired by Pâris's 1855 treatise [snippet only: Rijksmuseum models NG-MC-562 and NG-MC-564-1 — https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/collection/NG-MC-562].
  - Maudslay lifting-screw stern model, c.1850 [snippet only: Science Museum — https://collection.sciencemuseum.org.uk/objects/co39567].
  - [observed] The Napoléon model shows an open frame or aperture between the sternpost and the rudder, i.e. a propeller aperture.
- **Engine and masts:** Napoléon had a geared 2-cylinder Indret engine of 960 nhp [src: Shipscribe 133a; Navypedia]. No source was found on whether the mainmast was stepped on a platform over the shaft or engine room (see Gaps).
- **Boats:** Napoléon is credited with a chaloupe 12 × 3.75 × 1.30 m, a grand canot, a canot-major 15.50 × 2.50 × 0.82 m, a youyou and 2 baleinières [snippet only: Military Wiki, Napoléon (1850) infobox — https://military-history.fandom.com/wiki/French_ship_Napol%C3%A9on_(1850)]. The 15.5 m canot-major length looks suspicious, possibly 10.50 m. Stowage position was not found (see Gaps).

## 6. Paint and decoration, 1845–1860

- [observed] **Suffren model** (MnM 19 MG 5, 1829): black hull. Each battery row has a **light ochre/natural-wood band** with **black port lids**, giving a "chequer" effect. The rails are light wood and the lower hull is dark (copper or black).
- [observed] **Napoléon model** (MnM 23 MG 8, 1850): black wales and topsides with **two light ochre/natural-wood battery bands**, black port lids and black funnels. The underwater body is natural wood, probably the model's finish rather than paint.
  The light bands may be varnished wood standing in for white or yellow ochre. Period paintings (Commons category, §7) should settle white versus ochre.
- A modelling guide based on Boudriot gives the "usual colours for a French Royal navy ship" as white below the waterline, black above including the wales, and yellow ochre above the wales. This applies to the late 18th century [snippet only — https://timberandsail.com/wp-content/uploads/how-to-paint-historically-accurate-17th-18th-century-ships.pdf; derived from Boudriot's *Le Coureur*].
- France banned white as an external colour only in 1886, which implies white features were in use before then [snippet only: forum-marinearchiv.de — https://forum-marinearchiv.de/smf/index.php?msg=70415].
- Figurehead and stern decoration of the Second Republic and Second Empire: **not found** (see Gaps). Navypedia notes the ship was ex-*24 Février*, named after the 1848 revolution [src]. Any first decoration may therefore have been republican and replaced under the Empire.

## Conflicts and gaps

### Conflicts

1. **Sail area.**
   - Shipscribe gives Napoléon 3,411 sq yd (≈2,852 m²).
   - Wikipedia gives the Algésiras class 2,010 m².
   - Wikipedia gives the sailing Suffren 3,114 m².

   Either the Algésiras rig was reduced, or the figures measure different things.
2. **Napoléon length and beam on fr.wikipedia.** The infobox gives 77.8 m × 17 m, against 71.23–71.5 m and 16.8 m elsewhere. 77.8 m is probably overall length including the head. Do not mix these figures.
3. **22 cm shell gun weight and shell.** The barrel is given as 3,400 kg or 3,600 kg, the shell as 23.12 kg or 30 kg, and the length as 2.80 m or 2.85 m (Wikipedia Paixhans page, internal disagreement).
4. **30-pdr "short" vs "No.2".** The Wikipedia "short" gun has a 14.3-calibre bore, while Navypedia's No.2 is 165/15. They may be different pieces (No.2 vs No.3). The Wikipedia figures are for the 1820s–1840 system, and Napoléon may have carried the 1849 numbered series.
5. **Algésiras breadth.** Wikipedia gives 16.8 m and Navypedia 16.2 m. Navypedia's figure is probably moulded breadth (Shipscribe moulded = 16.22 m).
6. **Napoléon's boat dimensions** (Military Wiki): the canot-major at 15.50 m is longer than the chaloupe at 12 m, which is unlikely and probably a typo.
