## 2. Hull

- **Construction**: wooden hull of oak frames, plated over with wrought iron [EN-Gloire, FR] (opened). Dupuy de Lôme wanted iron but iron supply forced wood. Gloire was the only one of the three built with seasoned timber, so she lasted until 1879; Invincible and Normandie rotted [EN-Class] (opened). The hull below water was copper-sheathed [NavEnc] (opened, secondary). The Musée model shows a red-brown bottom, which fits copper.
- **Napoléon lines?** Many popular accounts call Gloire a cut-down two-decker (Napoléon type) with one battery removed. Battesti, via FR, says Dupuy de Lôme offset the ~820 t of armour by deleting one battery (opened). This means the design concept was a one-battery frigate derived from the screw liners. I found **no source saying the hull lines were literally copied from Napoléon**, so treat that as unverified.
- **Tumblehome**: modest. The Musée model front-view photo (§8) shows sides close to vertical above the waterline, with a slight inward slope toward the upper deck (photo reading). No numeric figure was found.
- **Bow**: a straight, near-vertical stem with a small cutwater and knee to carry the bowsprit. **No ram and no figurehead** appear on the NARA plan, the 1863 photo or the Musée model (plan and photo reading). The forefoot is rounded. NavEnc says "ram ... stepped ... rounded below WL" (opened, secondary). I could not confirm any projecting ram, so model a plain stem.
- **Stern**: a round stern with a counter overhanging the rudder. The plan shows two rows of cabin lights (cross-barred windows) in the counter and quarter. The rudder is large and rounded and hangs abaft the screw aperture (plan reading). There is no stern gallery [NavEnc] (opened).
- **Decks**, from bottom to top:
  - hold, with orlop/platform about 1.1 m below LWL (plan reading);
  - **battery (gun) deck** about 1.1 m above LWL, with about 2.25 m deck-to-deck to the upper deck (plan reading);
  - **upper (spar) deck** at the side about 3.4 m above LWL;
  - bulwark rail midships about 5.4 m above LWL (plan reading). This matches the "armour 5.4 m above WL" figure.
- **Forecastle and poop**: both raised.
  - The forecastle is about 15 m long, from the stem aft to about 0.19 L (plan reading). It is enclosed by a solid bulwark and carries the catheads and anchor gear.
  - The poop is about 16 m long, from the stern forward to about 0.80 L from the bow (plan reading). It has an open rail with stanchions and a row of small square lights in its side (1863 photo).
  - The waist between them has a high bulwark with no guns [NavEnc] (opened).
- **Conning tower / command post**: on the poop or quarterdeck, just forward of the mizzen. It is open-topped, 100 mm thick, and "over 3.7 m high". It has walkway wings and two ladders [EN-Class, citing Campbell; NavEnc] (opened). Shipscribe's photo caption says "the conning tower forward of the mizzen mast" (opened). On the NARA plan a raised platform with a rail sits about 0.66–0.72 L from the bow (plan reading).
- **Colour scheme** (from images; no textual source found):
  - Hull very dark: black in the Roux watercolour and the photos; dark brown-grey in the Le Breton lithograph; dark grey on the model.
  - **No white band or chequer.** Ports are shown as plain dark squares.
  - Masts and spars buff/ochre (Le Breton, Musée model).
  - Funnel buff/yellow with a black top (Musée model, Roux); Le Breton shows it brownish-ochre with a separate steam pipe.
  - Copper or red-brown below the waterline (model).
  - *(image reading only)*

## 4. Armament

**As built, 1860**: 36 × Mle 1858 rifled muzzle-loaders, nominally a 30-pdr.

- **Calibre**: 164.7 mm per EN-Class (opened), 164 mm per EN-Gloire (opened), 160 mm per FR (opened), 165/17 per Navypedia (opened).
- **Shell**: 44.9 kg at 322 m/s [EN-Class, citing Gardiner p.159] (opened).
- **Distribution**: EN-Class, citing Gille p.23 (opened), gives:
  - battery deck: 14 broadside guns per side (28), plus 2 pivot guns forward and 4 aft as chase guns, making 34;
  - upper deck: 2 chase guns, on the forecastle per Scientific American.
- **Scientific American 1861** (via NavEnc, opened, secondary): 34 battery guns about 6 ft above the sea, 2 long guns on the forecastle, and an "iron redoubt" on the quarterdeck.

**Gunports**

- The NARA plan shows **17 regularly spaced ports per side** (plan reading).
- **Spacing**: about 3.5 m centre to centre.
- **Port opening**: about 1.0 m wide × 0.93 m high.
- **Position**: the foremost port centre is about 12.0 m abaft the stem; the aftmost is about 68 m.
- **Why 17**: 14 broadside guns plus pivot guns working through the end ports gives 17 per side, which fits Gille's 34 battery guns. The reconciliation is my inference.
- **Photos**:
  - 1863 Rijksmuseum photo: 17 regular ports plus one extra opening at each end (lights).
  - "1869" photo: about 17–18 visible, partly hidden by a boat sail.
  - Development of Navies drawing: 15 (schematic).
  - NIE 1905 profile: about 13 (schematic).
- **Port lids / chase ports**: not described in sources. The forecastle bulwark carried the 2 upper-deck chase guns.

## 5. Propulsion

- **Engine**: one horizontal return-connecting-rod (HRCR) engine, two cylinders, single expansion. Navypedia says "single-expansion" (opened); EN-Class says "compound" (opened), which is probably an error; FR says "two-cylinder horizontal" (opened).
- **Builder**: Forges et Chantiers de la Méditerranée, Marseille, per Scientific American via NavEnc (opened, secondary).
- **Power**:
  - 2,500 ihp design [EN, citing Campbell] (opened);
  - 2,699 hp on trial [Navypedia] (opened);
  - "900 ch" nominal in the FR text [FR] (opened) and "900 hp" in Scientific American. This is nominal horsepower.
- **Boilers**: 8 oval boilers (Indret) [EN-Class, citing Gille; FR] (opened).
- **Funnel**: one, placed **between the fore and main masts**.
  - Position: about 0.44 L from the bow (plan reading).
  - Size: short and wide, about 2.6 m diameter. On the NARA plan its top is about 7.3 m above LWL, about 1.9 m above the rail. The 1863 photo also shows a short, squat light-coloured funnel.
  - Later or artistic images show it taller (Le Breton, Development of Navies). It may have been telescopic, but that is unconfirmed.
  - Deck around it: a casing with ventilation slats about 4 m long, a separate steam pipe, and four cowl vents [NavEnc] (opened).
- **Screw**:
  - One shaft, **six blades, 5.8 m diameter** [EN-Class, citing de Balincourt p.14] (opened); FR also says six-bladed (opened).
  - NavEnc says "in profile looks two-bladed ... photos show four" (opened, secondary). The NARA plan draws a two-bladed profile in the aperture.
  - **Hoisting**: not confirmed. The plan shows a tall aperture between the sternpost and rudder post, but I saw no lifting well.
- **Speed**:
  - 13.5 kn on official trials, 20–21 Aug 1860 [EN-Gloire; FR, citing Battesti] (opened);
  - 13.1 kn maximum, with another report saying no more than 11.75 kn and 11 kn practical [EN-Gloire] (opened);
  - 12.3 kn [Navypedia] (opened);
  - 13 kn infobox (opened).
- **Coal**: 675 t [EN-Class] (opened).
- **Range**: 4,000 km at 8 kn [EN-Gloire] (opened); the EN-Class infobox gives 2,200 nmi (opened).

## 6. Rig

- **Original 1860**: light rig of **1,096–1,100 m²**.
  - EN calls it a "barquentine" [EN-Class, citing Campbell] (opened). FR calls it a "trois-mâts goélette", a three-masted schooner [FR, citing Battesti] (opened).
  - The 1859 Le Breton lithograph shows three pole-like masts with gaffs (fore-and-aft) and no conspicuous square yards (image reading).
  - The Roux watercolour (1859–61) and the Musée model show square topsails on the fore and main, a fore-and-aft mizzen, and gaffs on all three masts (image reading). That is a barquentine or barque in practice.
- **Intermediate**: trois-mâts barque of **1,621 m²** [FR, citing Bernard 2019 p.80] (opened).
- **Full ship rig**: **2,508 m²** (27,000 sq ft) [EN-Gloire, FR] (opened); about 2,500 m² per Navypedia (opened). It was later "much reduced" because of rolling [EN-Class, Navypedia] (opened). NavEnc's legacy section claims the final rig had "only the foremast fully rigged" (opened, secondary).
- **Dates**: no source gives a year for any rig change.
  - The 1863 Rijksmuseum photo shows three masts with lower masts and topmasts and lower yards on all three. The trucks are about 33–39 m above WL (rough photo reading).
  - The "1869" photo shows longer masts (trucks about 37–47 m above WL, rough reading) with lower yards on fore, main and mizzen and topmasts up.
  - From this I infer the fuller ship rig by about 1864–69. This is inference only.
- **Mast positions from the stem**, as fractions of hull length (NARA plan reading, length ≈80.3 m):

| Mast | Fraction of L | Distance from stem |
|---|---|---|
| Fore | 0.242 | ≈19.4 m |
| Funnel | 0.438 | ≈35.2 m |
| Main | 0.549 | ≈44.1 m |
| Mizzen | 0.746 | ≈59.9 m |

  - Cross-checks:
    - Development of Navies profile: 0.27 / 0.47 / 0.57 / 0.76.
    - "1869" photo: 0.26 / 0.46 / 0.57 / 0.78.
    - 1863 photo: 0.22 / 0.41 / 0.54 / 0.75, with perspective error.
  - Lower-mast diameter on the plan is about 0.7 m (mizzen).
- **Bowsprit**: a bowsprit and jib-boom steeved up about 15–20° appear on the plan and photos. The Le Breton print and the model show a jib and headsails.
- **Yard counts per mast**: not found in text. Model and paintings suggest fore and main each with a course and topsail (and a topgallant on the full rig), and a mizzen with a gaff spanker plus a crossjack yard in the later photos.

## 7. Crew, boats, anchors

- **Crew**: 570 [EN, FR, Navypedia] (opened); "nearly 600" [NavEnc] (opened).
- **Boats**: about a dozen [NavEnc] (opened, secondary).
  - Whalers and utility barges abaft the funnel, handled by a boom.
  - Two yawls and two pinnaces aft around the poop.
  - The 1863 and 1869 photos show one large boat on stern-quarter davits (photo reading).
- **Anchors**: three, two forward and one aft under a davit [NavEnc] (opened, secondary).
  - The NARA plan shows a large stocked anchor stowed vertically on the forecastle side abaft the cathead.
  - It also shows two hawse holes close together near the stem, at about the level of the battery port tops (plan reading).

### How the NARA plan was measured (plan reading)

**Image and orientation.** I used the Commons 6000-px-wide rendition and measured in pixels with PIL crops and rulers. The stern is on the left and the bow on the right.

**Scale.** I calibrated it two ways:
- (a) Plan draught. The table's 7.830 m covers LWL to the bottom of the false keel, about 555 px midships, giving **≈71 px/m**.
- (b) Hull length. 77.9 m Navypedia/Conway LWL over about 5,600 px of LWL gives **≈72 px/m**.

I used 71.5 px/m (about 178 px/m on the full 14,922-px original). Expected error is about ±2 %.

Two independent cross-checks support the scale:
- Port sill to LWL reads 133 px = 1.86 m, against the published 1.88 m.
- Rail to LWL reads 5.4 m, against the published armour top of 5.4 m above WL.

**Readings at 71.5 px/m:**
- **Overall hull** (taffrail at x≈112 to stem head at x≈5850): 5,738 px ≈ **80.3 m**.
- **LWL**: ≈78.3 m.
- **Gunports**: 17 squares from x≈982 to x≈4995, regular pitch 251 px = **3.5 m**. Each is about 73 × 67 px (≈1.0 × 0.93 m). The sill is at y≈602 against LWL y≈735 midships.
- **Battery deck** (dashed line) ≈1.1 m above LWL.
- **Upper-deck dashed line** ≈3.4 m above LWL.
- **Rail** ≈5.4 m above LWL.
- **Lower armour edge**: a solid line ≈2.1 m below LWL.
- **Masts**, by centres of the double lines at the top of the sheet: mizzen x≈1570, main x≈2700, fore x≈4460.
- **Funnel**: x≈3240–3428, top at y≈217, so ≈7.3 m above LWL.
- **Trim**: the LWL slopes about 1 m by the stern relative to the keel. I read draughts of ≈8.0 m aft and ≈7.0 m fore. These are uncertain because the paper may also be slightly rotated; the keel line itself slopes about 0.6° on the image.

**Photo readings.** Mast and funnel fractions were measured on the 1863 and "1869" photos the same way, from stem to taffrail in pixels. Perspective gives about ±3 % error.

## 9. Sisters: Invincible and Normandie

- Same design, built of **unseasoned** timber.
  - Invincible was laid down 1 May 1858 at Toulon, launched 4 Apr 1861, completed Mar 1862, condemned 1872 [EN-Class] (opened).
  - Normandie was laid down 14 Sep 1858 at Cherbourg, launched 10 Mar 1860, completed 13 May 1862. She was the first ironclad to cross the Atlantic (Mexico, 1862) and was condemned 1871 [EN-Class, EN-Norm] (opened).
- EN-Norm gives Normandie's length as **77.25 m** and displacement as 5,650 t. All other figures are identical to Gloire's (opened), and nothing on the page fills a Gloire gap.
- Refits, from Navypedia (opened):
  - Invincible: 32 × 165/15 BL in 1864.
  - Normandie: 12 × 194 smoothbore + 16 × 165/15 BL in 1865.
  - Both: 8 × 240 + 6 × 194 in 1868.
- Normandie's engine was later reused in the monitor *Tonnerre* [NavEnc] (opened, secondary).

**Rig.** For a "first ironclad" look, use the **1860 barquentine**: square fore course and topsail on the foremast (plus a main topsail as on the model), gaff sails on all three masts, about 1,100 m². For a mid-1860s fleet look, use the **later full ship rig** of 2,508 m² with three square-rigged masts. The barquentine is lighter and closer to the launch-era ship; the full rig is better documented in photos.
