**Voxel scale (0.1 m)** [inf]: 313 long × 38 (straight) or 45 (flared) wide. Deck ~10 voxels above WL, lower oarports ~4 above WL, floor ~9 below WL, keel bottom ~10–11 below WL.

## 2. Hull shape and construction

- **Sheer / end heights**: no Byzantine figure found. Analogue: an Angevin galley contract (13th c.), cited by Pryor. The poop "rises in height" 3.60 m and the prow 2.99 m. The datum is unclear from the snippet; probably above the keel line at the ends [GB] p. 436.
  - Scaled to 75 % → poop ~2.7 m, prow ~2.25 m [inf]. This is consistent with prow ≤ 1.5 m above WL if the WL sits ~1 m up the stem [inf].
  - Miniatures (Madrid Skylitzes) draw the galleys as a crescent "banana boat" with both ends curving up [GB] p. 636. The image itself agrees [read], see §8.
- **Ram vs spur**: no waterline ram. Instead an **above-water spur** (*peronion*; *katakorax* is its coupling), "suspended by a chain or coupling from the stempost" head. It was not an integral part of the hull [GB] pp. 203, 208–209, glossary p. lvi. Purpose: ride over and break the enemy's oars, not hole the hull [GB] pp. 143–144.
  - The 949 Crete inventory lists 20 spurs for 20 dromons [GB] p. 209.
  - Late-antique pictures show a beam projecting forward from the stempost "about half way between the water and the gunwale", raked slightly upward [GB] p. 140.
  - Genoa 1267 contract: two oak galley spurs, width 0.25 m (the length is cut off in the snippet) [GB] p. 203. Grokipedia gives the Genoese spur as 10.42 m [weak, unverified].
- **Stem**: once the ram was gone, the stem and bow could be raked and flared [GB] p. 143.
- **Construction method**: by the 10th c., skeleton (frame-first) construction had replaced shell-first mortise-and-tenon. Seams were caulked and pitched [GB] pp. 145–152 via Wikipedia [read]; TMR review [read]. INA reports that all Yenikapı ships were built on frames [snip].
- **Yenikapı 16 galley** (Istanbul Univ.). Kocabaş & Özsait-Kocabaş, *Archaeonautica* 21 (2021), open access: https://journals.openedition.org/archaeonautica/3684 [read]
  - Preserved 22.5 × 2.4 m (starboard side only). **Estimated original 28–30 m × ~4 m.** Single-banked, **25 oars per side**. Radiocarbon date AD 720–890.
  - **Keel** 16 cm moulded × 14 cm sided (plane wood). A curved keel timber is scarfed on at the bow. Keelson preserved 6.04 m.
  - **Frames**: 139 stations, 5 cm sided × 6 cm moulded. **Room-and-space 20–24 cm**. Floors alternate with paired half-frames, and the futtocks are not fastened to the floors.
  - **Planks** 3 cm thick, 24–30 cm wide (black pine). Scarfs 60–105 cm.
  - **Wale 1** 13 × 15 cm, with 16 thwart sockets at **90–97 cm centres** (= rowing room). The oarport strake was probably at the 3rd wale.
  - Stringers 16–20 × 3 cm (fir).
- **Yenikapı galleys in general**: six galleys in all. YK2 and YK4 (INA, Pulak); YK13, YK16, YK25 and YK36 (Istanbul Univ.).
  - "about 100 feet (30 m) long and 13 feet (4 m) wide" (Pulak, via NBC News) [read] https://www.nbcnews.com/news/amp/wbna56700668
  - The Istanbul Univ. galleys have preserved lengths of 19–23 m [read, YK16 paper].
  - Woods: plane or oak keels, plane or elm floors, black pine / cedar / chestnut planks [read] https://avesis.iuc.edu.tr/yayin/9cdbb464-c89d-4a0b-b963-bbde01d20624/woods-of-the-old-galleys-of-yenikapi-istanbul
  - Midships the galleys are flat-floored [snip].
  - These are **monoremes (galea type)**, smaller than the bireme dromon. **No YK2/YK4 scantlings found**: the Pulak et al. 2015 IJNA paper (doi 10.1111/1095-9270.12083) returned 403.
- **Olympias (trireme), construction analogue only**: 36.9 m long, 5.5 m beam, 1.25 m draught, 47 t. Shell-first pegged mortise-and-tenon [read] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympias_(trireme). Pryor's "Olympias Mark II" figures: 34.5 m without ram, 5.45 m beam over the outriggers, room 0.98 m [GB] p. 291.

## 3. Deck and oar arrangement

- **Full deck** (*katastrōma*) [GB] p. 127 via Wikipedia [read]. Leo VI's dromon is a bireme:
  - **lower bank** of **25 oars per side, rowed seated below deck** through oarports (*trēmata*) in a strake called the *thyreon*, from tholes (*skalmoi*) [GB] pp. 278, 285, 287;
  - **upper bank** of 25 per side rowed **from above the deck**. These upper oarsmen also fought as marines [GB] pp. 232, 255; Wikipedia [read].
  - Total 100 oars. The 949 inventory issued 120 oars per dromon (spares) [GB] pp. 285, 291.
- **Room (interscalmium)**: **~1.0 m** [GB] pp. 287–291, 435. Upper and lower oarsmen are staggered by ~½ room [GB] p. 291. Yenikapı thwarts are at 0.90–0.97 m [read].
- **Lower oars**:
  - length **3.85 m**, of which 1.045 m inboard and 2.805 m outboard [GB] pp. 292–293;
  - blade ~0.81 m; the shaft crosses the waterline 0.96 m from the tip; **thole ~0.365 m above WL**; angle to the water ~11° [GB] p. 294.
  - Leather sleeves sealed the ports [GB] p. 281.
- **Upper oars**:
  - angle to the water ≥ ~28° [GB] p. 443; probably longer than the lower oars [GB] pp. 298, 304;
  - their tholes sit ~16 cm (scaled oars) to ~47 cm (full-size oars) outboard of the lower tholes [GB] pp. 298, 300;
  - the hands of upper and lower oarsmen are ≥ ~1.40 m apart [GB] p. 288. The context suggests vertical separation.
- **Bulwark / pavesade** (*kastellōma*): marines hang their shields along it. The upper hull had to be near-vertical to take the shields [GB] pp. 282–283, 298.
- **Voxel layout** [inf]: 25 lower ports per side at 1.0 m (10-voxel) centres, starting ~2.5–3 m aft of the stem. Port bottoms ~4 voxels above WL; a 2×2 opening suits the ~15 cm sleeves. Upper-bank tholes go on the gunwale above the deck, offset ½ room (5 voxels).

## 4. Rig

- **Two masts**, lateen. The **larger one is forward** (foremast) and the second is amidships [GB] p. 238; TMR review p. 238 [read]. Leo's xylokastra stand "around the middle mast", which implies at least two masts [GB] p. 238.
- **Mast lengths** (Pryor scales Angevin galleys to 75 %):
  - **foremast ~11.85 m**, masthead **~10.65 m above WL**;
  - **midships mast ~8.3 m** [GB] p. 244.
  - Angevin originals: foremast 15.82 m **raked forward ~13°**, masthead 14.20 m above WL; middle mast 11.075 m [GB] p. 243.
- **Yards**: **foresail ~20.17 m "on the yard", midships sail ~15.43 m**. The peak of the foresail is ~21 m above WL [GB] p. 248. Angevin equivalents: 26.89 m and 20.57 m.
- **Sail area**: none found.
- **Mast positions as a fraction of length**: **not found**.
  - Suggestion: foremast at ~0.25 L from the stem (raked 13° forward), midships mast at ~0.5 L [inf, low]. The castles stand beside the midships mast [GB] pp. 234–236.
- **Handling**:
  - masts were lowered aft before battle, onto mast crutches; there were yard crutches on deck too [GB] pp. 218, 231, 250–252;
  - no fore- or backstays with a lateen rig [GB] p. 218;
  - 9th-c. pictures show mastheads "hooked/beaked" forward to hang the yard ahead of the mast [GB] pp. 245–246.

## 5. Steering

- **Two quarter rudders** (*pēdalia*), each with blade, shaft (*auchēn*) and tiller (*oiax*). They sat in box housings on the outside of the hull at the poop, on through-beams [GB] pp. 215, 220–226; Wikipedia [read].
  - "Huge blades with extremely long shafts": hoisted to the mastheads, they could serve as bearers for siege bridges [GB] pp. 225, 240.
- **Size**: none found. Suggestion: shaft ~5–6 m, blade ~2 m × 0.4–0.5 m, hung at the quarters ~2–3 m forward of the sternpost [inf, low].
- The poop had a berth (*krabattos*) for the captain under a tent or awning on a wooden frame (*skēnē*) [GB] p. 215.

## 6. Armament and fittings

- **Bow siphon**: Leo VI, *Taktika* §6: a siphon "in front at the prow, bound in bronze" [GB] pp. 487, 575.
  - Above it is the **pseudopation**, a raised false floor of planks fortified with planks all round. Marines stood on it to fight [GB] pp. 203, 487.
  - Extra siphons could go amidships and astern (941) [GB] pp. 203, 618 via Wikipedia "Greek fire" [read] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_fire
- **What the siphon looked like**:
  - Haldon & Byrne 1977: bronze force-pump, a brazier pre-heater (*propyron*) under a sealed tank, and a **bronze-covered nozzle on a swivel** (*strepton*). Iron heat shields (*boukolia*) appear in the inventories.
  - Haldon's 2002 full-scale test reached ~15 m range [read, Wikipedia citing Haldon 2006 and P&J pp. 621–628].
  - **Animal heads**: Anna Komnene (c. 1103) says gilded heads of lions and other beasts in brass or iron were fixed at the prows, and the fire came out of their mouths [read, Wikipedia citing Dawes p. 292]. This is late 11th c., not 10th.
  - The Skylitzes miniature shows a man at the bow holding a straight tube that spouts flame [read], see §8.
- **Hand siphon** (*cheirosiphōn*): Leo VI claims to have invented it. Haldon reads it as a simple syringe [read, Wikipedia citing P&J p. 617, 627].
- **Xylokastra (wooden castles)**: "fortified with planks, around the middle of the mast on the largest dromons". Men threw millstones and iron weights from them [GB] pp. 229, 487, 575.
  - Pryor: **two castles, one each side of the centreline, with a clear gangway between them** so that the mast and yard could be lowered and tacked. Castles nearer the bow would give better angles [GB] pp. 234–236.
  - Wikipedia: on either side between the masts (Pryor 1995 p. 104) [read].
  - **No dimensions found.** Suggestion: each ~2.5–3 m long × ~1.2 m wide, platform ~1.8–2 m above the deck, plank parapet ~1 m [inf, low].
- **Other fittings**:
  - bow-ballistae (*toxobalistrai*) at prow, stern and sides [GB] p. 379;
  - catheads (*periboloi*) on both sides near the prow for the anchors [GB] p. 535;
  - shields along the pavesade [GB] p. 282.
- **Crew** (brief):
  - *ousia* of 108 [read, Wikipedia citing P&J pp. 189–192];
  - large dromons ~200: 50 at the lower oars and 150 above deck able to fight [GB] p. 255;
  - *De cerimoniis* gives 230 oarsmen + 70 marines for the heavy dromon [read, Wikipedia].

## 7. Colours and appearance

- **Sails**: Prokopios (6th c.) says Belisarius's ships had sails "painted red from the upper corner for about a third of their length" as a recognition mark [GB] p. 153. This is the only sail-colour text found.
- **Hull**: the Leo VI inventory carries tow, pitch and liquid pitch [GB] pp. 152, 487. Seams were caulked and pitched → **black or very dark hull** [inf].
- **Signal flag**: a black *kamelaukion* raised on a pole [GB] pp. 398, 591.
- **Madrid Skylitzes "Greek fire" miniature** (12th-c. Sicilian copy) [read, viewed the image]:
  - hull black or very dark brown with a gold disc or rosette on the side;
  - ochre or gold mast and lateen yard;
  - pale oars;
  - bright orange-red flame from a bow tube;
  - crew in blue and grey.
  - Stylized: one bank of oars, crescent hull.
- **Gilded animal-head mouthpieces** (Anna Komnene, late 11th c.) [read].
- Nothing else found on hull paint.
