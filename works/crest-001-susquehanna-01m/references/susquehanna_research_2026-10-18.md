## 1. Identity and history

- **Type and designers.** Side-wheel steam frigate. John Lenthall, Chief Naval Constructor, designed the hull and rigging. Charles W. Copeland designed the engines and boilers. Murray & Hazlehurst of Baltimore built the machinery. [STUART p. 83, opened; BARTOL p. 2, opened; SCIAM, opened]
- **Builder (sources conflict).**
  - The contemporary sources say the Philadelphia Navy Yard. STUART says she was "commenced at the Philadelphia Navy Yard" in 1847 and launched on 6 April 1850. SCIAM (1853) also names Philadelphia. BARTOL's trial "first run from Philadelphia to Norfolk" is consistent with that.
  - The modern sources say the New York Navy Yard: DANFS (keel 1847, launched **5 April 1850**), WIKI and NavSource.
  - **I recommend Philadelphia, because the 1853 sources say so.** LOW on the exact launch day (5 or 6 April).
- **Construction.** The frame is live oak, planked with white oak and braced with wrought iron. [STUART p. 83, opened]
- **Commission and early service.** Commissioned 24 Dec 1850 under Capt. John H. Aulick [DANFS, opened]. She ran trials in Jan 1851 (Philadelphia to Norfolk) and sailed 8 June 1851 to become flagship of the East India Squadron [DANFS; STUART].
- **Uraga, 1853.**
  - She became Perry's flagship and entered Edo Bay on **8 July 1853** [DANFS, opened]. Commander Franklin Buchanan was her captain [HGRAM, opened].
  - Susquehanna and Mississippi steamed in towing the sloops Saratoga and Plymouth [LEWIS p. 133, opened]. The squadron anchored off Uraga at about 5 p.m. [HAWKS vol. 1 p. 232, opened].
  - Susquehanna led the line [LEWIS p. 133]. The squadron left on 17 July and returned on 12/13 Feb 1854 [DANFS; HGRAM].
- **Paint and the "black ship" name.**
  - Pictures of her own hull:
    - de Simone's 1857 painting (img 03) shows the hull as **dark, almost uniform**, with no clear contrasting stripe.
    - The 1853 Japanese watercolour (img 01) shows a black hull. A light band runs at the bulwark/port line, with white hammock-netting covers above.
  - The near-sister Powhatan in the Stuart 1853 plate (img 05) has a **black hull with one thin light stripe** at about the upper-wale / port-sill level.
  - The Mississippi plate in STUART **(M)** also shows a light stripe.
  - Why "black": Dower (MIT) thinks the name came "probably from the color of their hulls". Wikipedia's "Black Ships" article says the term predates Perry, from pitch-blackened Portuguese carracks [snippet: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Ships ; https://visualizingcultures.mit.edu/black_ships_and_samurai/bss_essay03.html]. ja.wikipedia says the hull was coated with pitch, but cites no source (LOW).
- **Figurehead and cutwater.**
  - NARA holds a c. 1850 pencil design, 13 × 24 in, from the Records of the Bureau of Ships. It is captioned for **both** Susquehanna and Powhatan. It shows an **eagle-like bird at the head of the cutwater, with a trailing garland of flowers, fruit and acorns** along the trailboard. The sheet is numbered "108-1-7A". [NARA-FH, opened; img 06]
  - In the Gutekunst 1860s photo (img 02) the head looks like a carved head with trailboards, but it is too small to identify. LOW.
- **Stern decoration and name board.** Not found.
- **Later history.** Mediterranean 1856–58. In the Civil War she was at Hatteras, Port Royal, Sewell's Point and Fort Fisher. Decommissioned 14 Jan 1868, sold 27 Sep 1883. [DANFS, opened]

## 3. Hull form

- **Decks.** Note G (for Powhatan and Susquehanna) names a keel, an **orlop deck**, a **berth deck** and a **gun deck** [STUART App. pp. 171–175, opened].
  - No separate spar deck is specified, so the gun deck appears to be the weather deck.
  - The bulwark is built on it: "Height of top of plank sheer, above deck, 2 feet" (p. 173).
  - LOW: the weather-deck interpretation is my inference.
- **Deck heights (Note G, p. 175).** The text reads "Height in the clear under the beam, 7 feet; under the gun-deck beam to top of berth-deck plank, 6 feet 9 inches; … top of berth-deck beam to orlop plank, 5 feet 2 inches". The first figure (7 ft) is ambiguous about which deck it means.
- **Deck scantlings.** Gun-deck plank 4¾ in yellow pine. Berth-deck beams sided 1 ft. Guard beams sided 1 ft 8 in, moulded 2 ft 2 in. The rudder head is 2 ft in diameter, with 4 pintles. There are two watertight bulkheads, at the forward fire-room and at the after end of the engine room. [STUART App., opened]
- **Gunports.**
  - Per side: not found in text.
  - Air-ports run "between the second and third strake" below the gun-deck clamps. [STUART App. p. 172, opened]
  - Pictures:
    - de Simone 1857 (img 03): a few small ports forward of the paddle box. The hull aft is plain except for the boats.
    - Japanese 1853 watercolour (img 01): about 6–7 small square ports per side, forward and aft of the wheel. LOW.
- **Freeboard.** Not given. Estimate: depth of hold 26.5 ft + keel about 1.5 ft − draft 18.5 ft ≈ **9–10 ft to the gun deck at the side**, plus about 2 ft of plank sheer and a higher bulwark above that. This is the designer's estimate, LOW.
- **Sheer.** Gentle in every picture. No figure found.
- **Bow.** Cutwater with a figurehead and trailboards [NARA-FH; img 02].
- **Stern.**
  - Gutekunst (img 02): a rounded or elliptical counter with stern davits. LOW.
  - The modern cutaway by K. Tanii (MIT) shows a round stern with stern windows. This is a modern interpretation, not evidence.
  - Quarter galleries: none visible in any image.
- **Bulwarks.** A continuous solid bulwark with hammock rails. White hammock covers show in the Japanese watercolour (img 01) and in the Powhatan plate (img 05).

## 4. Propulsion

- **Engines.** "Two inclined, direct-acting, condensing engines, with inclined air-pumps" [STUART p. 83, opened]. BARTOL also says "Two Inclined Engines" [opened]. There is **no walking beam**.
- **Cylinders.** **70 in bore × 10 ft stroke** [STUART: "70 inches", "10 feet"; BARTOL: 5 ft 10 in × 10 ft 0 in, opened].
  - Displacement of both pistons per stroke: 534.51 cu ft.
  - **The engines take up 49 ft fore and aft in the hull** [STUART, opened].
- **Operating figures.** About 12 rpm at about 10 lb steam, cut-off at 6 ft (half stroke) [BARTOL, opened].
- **Horsepower.** Nominal and indicated horsepower are not found in a primary source. ja.wikipedia's table gives 420 NHP / 795 IHP without a source (LOW).
- **Boilers.** **Four copper boilers, double-return ascending flues, "back to back"** [BARTOL; STUART, opened].
  - Each boiler is **15¾ ft long × 15 ft broad × 12¾ ft high** [STUART p. 84, opened].
  - Fire surface 8,652 sq ft; grate 342 sq ft. Boilers and fittings weigh 184 t.
- **Funnel (chimney).**
  - **One funnel**, standing on the boilers ("The steam chimney is attached to the boilers") [BARTOL note, opened].
  - Cross-section **54 sq ft**, which gives **≈8.3 ft diameter** if it is round (my calculation).
  - **Height 65 ft above the grates** [BARTOL; STUART, opened]. Taking the grates as roughly 20–25 ft below the weather deck, the top is about **40–45 ft above deck**. That conversion is the designer's estimate, LOW.
  - Colour: black in img 03 and img 05.
  - **Position:** img 03 (1857), img 05 (Powhatan 1852/53) and img 01 (Japanese 1853) all show the funnel **immediately forward of the paddle box**. In the Gutekunst photo (img 02) it appears over the after half of the box, probably a perspective effect. I recommend "just forward of the paddle shaft".
  - **(M)** Mississippi's funnel is **aft** of the wheels [STUART plate; baxleystamps].
- **Coal.** 900 t in the bunkers [STUART]. On trials she burned 3,270 lb of bituminous coal per hour [BARTOL].
- **Paddle wheels.** "Of the common radial kind", so **not feathering** [STUART p. 84, opened].

  | Wheel item | Value |
  |---|---|
  | Diameter, outside to outside | **31 ft 2 in** [BARTOL] / **31⅙ ft** [STUART] |
  | Paddle length (wheel width) | **9 ft 6 in** [BARTOL] / **9½ ft** [STUART] |
  | Paddle width | **34 in, double** (two boards of 17 in) |
  | Paddles per wheel | **26** |
  | Area of two paddles | 54 sq ft |
  | Immersion of lower edge at 18½ ft draft | **64 in** [STUART]. BARTOL's dip of 5 ft is corrected to **6 ft** in its errata [opened] |

  - Derived: axis about 10¼ ft above the waterline, wheel top about 25¾ ft above the waterline (my calculation).
  - **Fore-and-aft position of the wheels:** no text source.
    - Measured from images (LOW): Powhatan plate shaft at ≈0.53 of hull length from the bow; Napha print ≈0.50; de Simone ≈0.60; Gutekunst ≈0.37 (strong perspective).
    - Recommendation: **≈0.48–0.52 L from the bow**. This is the designer's choice.
- **Speed.**
  - DANFS gives 10 kn [opened]; NavSource gives 8 kn [opened].
  - Under sail alone, with the wheels turned slowly by steam, she averaged **8¼ kn over 20 days**. Sail-only with the wheels removed: mean about 6½ kn, best 7¾ kn [STUART p. 85, opened].

## 5. Rig

- **Barque-rigged**, "21,230 square feet of canvas in the principal sails" [STUART p. 83, opened]. She has three masts:
  - Fore and main are square-rigged.
  - The mizzen is fore-and-aft only; there are no yards on it in img 02 (1860s) or img 03 (1857).

- **Mast heights and spar dimensions:** not found.

## 6. Armament

- **July 1853: no primary source found.** What I have:
  - PINEAU's table (via MIT, opened): Susquehanna **6 shell guns + 3 "other"**, 9 in total; Mississippi 12 shell guns.
  - LEWIS: "steam frigate Susquehanna of 9 guns" (p. 126) [opened].
  - GLEASON (Feb 1853): 10 guns [opened].
  - HAWKS: Japanese visitors on board "observed the big gun and rightly styled it a 'Paixhan'" [opened]. This is generic, not a gun list.
  - A forum post says the official records show **three 10-in pivot guns, two on the forecastle and one at the stern** [snippet, shipsofscale; unverified, LOW].
- **Anachronism warning.** DANFS, NavSource, WIKI, and HGRAM (for 1853) give **2 × 150-pdr Parrott rifles, 12 × 9-in Dahlgrens and 1 × 12-pdr rifle**. That is a **Civil War battery**: Parrott rifles date from about 1860–61. Do not use it for 1853.
- **(M) Mississippi in 1853** [STUART p. 38, opened]: "two ten inch … Paixhan guns, mounted one on each bow, on pivots; and eight, eight inch … Paixhan guns, mounted abaft the wheels, in broadside".
- **Reconstruction for the model (designer's inference, LOW).** Three 10-in shell guns on pivots, two forward and one aft, plus six 8-in shell guns in broadside, three per side abaft the wheels. This fits PINEAU's 6 + 3 and the Mississippi pattern, with all guns on the weather deck.
- **Later changes.** The Civil War battery above. Catton (via a forum) gives fifteen 8-in guns + a 12-pdr in 1861 [snippet, LOW].

## 7. Crew, boats, anchors

- **Crew.** **300** [PINEAU via MIT, opened]; 350 [GLEASON, Feb 1853, opened]. NavSource and WIKI list it as unknown.
- **Boats.**
  - Count, types and stowage: not found in text.
  - de Simone 1857 (img 03): two large boats hang in davits on the quarter, between the main and mizzen masts and abaft the mizzen.
  - Gutekunst (img 02): quarter davits and stern davits.
  - Japanese watercolour (img 01): small boats over the bulwark.
  - No source shows boats over the paddle boxes for Susquehanna.
- **Anchors.** Not found. Note G mentions cat-heads (sided 1 ft 3 in) and cable bitts (1 ft 6 in square at the head) [STUART App. p. 174, opened].

## 9. Paddle boxes and deck fittings

- **Paddle-box decoration.**
  - The 1853 Japanese drawing shows a **radiating / sunburst slot pattern** on the box face.
  - The 1860s photo shows a **semicircular fan of radial slots (ventilation) above the axle, with horizontal louvre bands below**.
  - The Powhatan plate shows a similar arched fan.
  - [img 01, 02, 05]
- **Walking beam.** None: the engines are inclined direct-acting [STUART].
- **Between and around the boxes.**
  - The photo shows a **raised curved top / bridge-like casing over the box**, a **ventilator cowl abaft the funnel**, and a **deckhouse near the mainmast** (1860s; LOW for 1853).
  - de Simone shows the box top white (an awning or painted top?). LOW.
  - The hull "wheel-houses" (the paddle boxes) are framed on the guard beams [STUART App., opened].
- **Ship's bell and steering wheel position.** Not found.
